The problem

The warning is on the leaflet. The food is in your hand.

Food warnings on medicines are small print on a leaflet. Nothing connects them to the food you are actually holding.

Works today

What the app does right now

Everything in this section is built and running in the app.

1

Recall check

Looks a product up by barcode in CPSC, FDA food and FDA drug recall data, matching on the product's own code. It says "no recall found in the sources that answered", never "safe".

2

Food and medicine interactions

Joins what a food component does to a protein with what the medicine does to the same protein, and draws it. Grapefruit and simvastatin meet at CYP3A4; leafy greens and warfarin meet at VKORC1.

3

Where the drug binds

Experimental structures of simvastatin in HMG-CoA reductase (PDB 1HW9) and warfarin in VKORC1 (PDB 6WV3), drawn with py2Dmol.

4

Protein view and design brief

The target's predicted structure from AlphaFold DB in an interactive three.js view, plus an exportable brief for a design run.

5

Near me

Recalls distributed in your US state and CDC respiratory illness activity.

6

Watchlist

A scheduler re-checks saved products and medicines for new recalls.

7

Claude

Writes a plain-language summary from the retrieved evidence and answers questions by calling five live tools. The same tools ship as an MCP server and a Claude Code skill.

How it works

Three steps

  1. Scan

    Take or upload a photo of a barcode, or type the number. The app reads the code, identifies the product and checks it against recall data.

  2. See where they meet

    Add your medicines. The app shows the protein a food and a medicine share, and where the drug binds.

  3. Stay covered

    Save items to the watchlist. They are re-checked for new recalls on a schedule.

Data sources

Limits

What this is not

  • It shows information from public sources. It is not medical advice.
  • Designs would be research hypotheses, not treatments.
  • The app does not diagnose anyone.
